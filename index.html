About

Most people encounter proteins as pills. I encounter them as three-dimensional architectures — thousands of atoms arranged by evolution, each surface a potential drug target. That difference in perspective is what drives everything I do.

I'm a DPhil researcher at the Hong Kong Polytechnic University, working in computational structural biology. My research uses cryo-EM validated structures to understand protein-ligand interactions at atomic resolution — the level of detail that determines whether a drug candidate works or doesn't. I published my first paper on SARS-CoV-2 structural mechanisms in the Journal of Biological Chemistry while still an undergraduate at HKUST.

Outside the lab, I founded AtomBios to make molecular simulation accessible to drug teams who need answers in hours, not months. I've been selected by the Nobel Foundation, the Shaw Prize, and the Chinese Academy of Sciences. I've won gold at Hong Kong's largest deep-tech competition. But what I care about most is the work itself — understanding how molecules behave, and building tools that let others see what I see.

Currently: finishing my DPhil, building AtomBios toward its first pharma pilot, speaking at drug discovery symposiums across Asia.

AtomBios

Every drug team I talked to during my DPhil had the same frustration. They could see a protein structure, but couldn't test what happens when a molecule docks. The simulation tools existed, but they required a bioinformatics team and weeks of setup. I thought that was absurd.

So I built AtomBios. One-click molecular dynamics. A thousand binding modes tested before a single compound is synthesized. Built on cryo-EM validated structural data, designed for the teams who actually make drugs.

AtomBios is incubated at Hong Kong Science and Technology Park, with conditional funding approved through the HKSTP Ideation Programme.
