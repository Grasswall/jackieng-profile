I read the structures
that decide whether
a drug works.

Computational structural biology · DPhil researcher · Founder, AtomBios

Four pharma companies spent billions targeting the same pocket on a pain channel. Then someone solved the cryo-EM structure — and the drug actually binds 30 ångströms away from where every model predicted.

That's the kind of problem I work on. DPhil researcher at Hong Kong Polytechnic University. I take 3D coordinates from cryo-EM and X-ray crystallography, run molecular dynamics, and figure out what actually happens when a drug candidate meets a protein. Not what the model predicts. What the physics says.

1stauthor, J. Biol. Chem.
4.0GPA (of 4.3), HKUST
7×Dean's List

Outside the lab, I built AtomBios — because the teams actually making drugs shouldn't need six months of specialist setup to run a binding simulation. Selected by the Nobel Foundation, the Shaw Prize, and the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Right now: finishing my DPhil, getting AtomBios to its first pharma pilot, and talking about structures at conferences across Asia.

"Every drug team I talked to had the same setup. They could see a protein structure. They knew where the pocket was. But they couldn't test what happens when a molecule actually docks — not the static snapshot, the dynamics."
— on why I built AtomBios

So I built AtomBios. One click. Upload a structure, define a ligand, run molecular dynamics. A thousand binding modes tested before a single compound gets synthesised. The kind of simulation that used to take a specialist six months of setup now takes an afternoon.

AtomBios is incubated at Hong Kong Science and Technology Park, with conditional funding approved through the HKSTP Ideation Programme.
