Chapter 1

It started with a cryo-EM image

I didn't plan to become a computational biologist. At HKUST, I was just another biochemistry student trying to figure out what research actually meant.

Then I saw my first cryo-EM structure. A protein frozen mid-action, every atom in place. That's when it clicked: molecular mechanisms aren't abstract — they're architectural blueprints you can see.

I spent the next three years learning everything I could. Structural biology. Computational chemistry. Molecular dynamics. Graduated First Class Honours with a 4.0 GPA, made Dean's List 7 times, won the Kitchell Research Award.

But the real lesson came later: academic tools are built for precision, not speed. And pharma needs speed.

Chapter 3

I saw the gap pharma couldn't fix

In those rooms with Nobel Laureates and pharma executives, the same frustration kept surfacing: academic simulation tools are too slow for drug discovery timelines.

A medicinal chemist wants to test 1,000 binding hypotheses before committing synthesis budget. But setting up molecular dynamics takes weeks. The tools are built for Nature papers, not pipeline decisions.

I realized: someone who understands both the atomic-level science AND the pharma urgency could build the bridge. That's when AtomBios was born.

Chapter 4 — AtomBios

Molecular simulation at pharma speed

AtomBios is a computational biology platform that lets drug teams visualize protein–ligand interactions in real time. No weeks of setup. No IT tickets. Just: load your structure, test your hypothesis, get your answer.

  • One-click molecular dynamics — rigor without the ritual
  • Test 1,000 binding modes before you synthesize one
  • Built on cryo-EM validated structural data
  • Deployed at HK Science and Technology Park
