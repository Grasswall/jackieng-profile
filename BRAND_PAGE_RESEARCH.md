# Exceptional Personal Brand Page Research
**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**Subject:** Jackie Ng — Drug delivery researcher, structural biologist, AtomBios founder  
**Goal:** Create an exceptional personal brand website that positions Jackie for pharma R&D roles, research leadership, and investment opportunities

## Research Summary

I analyzed **40+ portfolio and personal brand sites** across three categories:
1. Award-winning creative portfolios (Awwwards, Webby winners 2024-2026)
2. Academic/scientist websites (12 annotated examples from PhD to faculty)
3. Biotech/pharma industry examples (founders, consultants, startup websites)

## Key Findings: What Makes Exceptional Personal Brand Pages

### 1. **First 3 Seconds Matter Most**

Exceptional sites answer three questions immediately:
- **Who are you?** (Name + photo/visual identity)
- **What do you do?** (One-line value proposition)
- **What can I do here?** (Clear navigation or CTA)

**Best examples:**
- **Jonathan Goodman (business coach):** "I help fitness professionals build online businesses" + two lead magnets in first screen
- **Perry Wang (product designer):** Clean hero with role + location, then straight to featured work
- **Tim Ferriss:** Clean layout with blog/podcast/books/newsletter visible immediately

**What NOT to do:**
- Generic "Welcome to my site"
- Resume-style timeline
- Scrolling hero without clear purpose

---

### 2. **Show Work, Not Claims**

The best sites are **indexes of evidence**, not marketing pages.

**Academic examples that work:**
- **Mayank Mittal (robotics, ETH/NVIDIA):** Each publication gets an **animated GIF teaser** + links to paper/code/project page. For robotics, motion > static images.
- **Nicole Paul (earthquake engineering):** Per-paper resource row: **PDF · code · data · DOI**. Open science done right.
- **Andrew Heiss (public policy):** Full teaching portfolio with standalone course websites + "Now" page (what I'm working on right now).

**What wins:**
- Projects with **process + outcome**, not just final product
- **Linked artifacts:** code repos, datasets, deployed demos
- **Case studies** over project grids (especially for strategic work)

**What loses:**
- "I'm an expert in X, Y, Z" with no proof
- Gallery of logos/clients without context
- Claims without measurable outcomes

---

### 3. **Freshness Signals Are Non-Negotiable**

Sites feel dead or alive based on **visible recency cues**:

✅ **What works:**
- Dated news feed (publications, talks, awards) updated within last 3 months
- Explicit "Last updated: [date]" (Martin Bulla, ornithologist)
- Recent blog posts or project timestamps
- "Now" page (Andrew Heiss) — one paragraph on current work

❌ **What kills trust:**
- Copyright © 2022 with no other dates
- Stale "Latest News" from 2023
- Undated portfolio (looks like abandoned site)

**The pattern:** Visitors check for signs of life BEFORE reading content.

---

### 4. **Two Types of Exceptional Design**

#### **Type A: Content-First Minimalism**
Clean, fast, work speaks for itself.

**Examples:**
- **Seth Godin:** Blog-first site. Daily posts. Zero decoration. Content is king.
- **Ben Kuhn:** Plain text, sensible headings, actual writing. No "brand."
- **Patrick Collison:** Index of projects/essays. Proof of doing, not marketing.

**When it works:**
- Your work is genuinely interesting (research, essays, projects)
- Audience is technical/research-oriented
- You want to be taken seriously, not "cool"

#### **Type B: Experience-Driven Design**
Design IS the portfolio. Aesthetic novelty signals creativity.

**Award-winning examples:**
- **Jessica Walsh (designer):** Portfolio is a visual statement. Design proves design skill.
- **Rubens Cantuni:** GIF of "CIAO" being edited on load. Shows skill immediately.
- **Ine Agresta:** Bold colors, animated logo, playful interactions. Design = personality.

**When it works:**
- You're selling creativity/design/visual skill
- Target audience values aesthetics (agencies, brand work)
- Your differentiation IS your visual identity

**The mistake:** Trying to do both badly. Pick one lane.

### 5. **The Biotech/Pharma Constraint**

Industry-specific findings from biotech/pharma sites:

**What pharma/biotech audiences expect:**
- **Credibility over creativity:** Clean, professional, no gimmicks
- **Science-first:** Publications, structures, data before personality
- **Industry signals:** Funding, partnerships, regulatory milestones
- **Trust cues:** Affiliations, advisors, peer recognition

**Best biotech examples:**
- **BioNTech:** Blue/green palette (industry standard), investor info prominent, clean tabs for therapeutic areas
- **Vinta Bio:** Founders on homepage (personal + professional), warm tone breaks biotech sterility
- **Akouos:** Emotional connection through mission, empathetic tone

**What doesn't work in pharma:**
- Over-designed "agency" aesthetic
- Marketing claims without data
- Hiding credentials/affiliations

### 6. **Academic Portfolio Patterns**

From 12 annotated academic sites:

**Universal elements:**
- **ORCID, Google Scholar, GitHub** links (infrastructure, not decoration)
- **Featured publications** with artifacts (code/data/project pages)
- **CV download** (PDF, always accessible)
- **Clear affiliation** (current institution + lab/group)

**What separates strong from weak:**
- **Curation > Completeness:** "Featured work" beats exhaustive lists
- **Narrative thread:** Not just "what" but "why" — research direction, story
- **Maintenance:** Updated news feed > stale copyright date

**PhD-specific needs:**
- Show trajectory (where you're going, not just where you've been)
- Prove productivity despite no faculty position yet
- Signal you're active (conferences, preprints, collaborations)

---

### 7. **The "Now" Page Pattern**

**What it is:** One paragraph on what you're currently working on.

**Why it works:**
- Beats stale "Latest News" feed
- Shows you're active without constant updates
- Personal + professional in one honest statement

**Example (Andrew Heiss):**
> "I'm currently working on X project, teaching Y course this semester, and wrapping up a paper on Z. Next up: [upcoming conference/deadline]."

**Update frequency:** Monthly or when major milestones change.

---

### 8. **Call-to-Action Hierarchy**

Every page should have a **primary next step**.

**For Jackie's audiences:**

| Audience | Primary CTA | Secondary CTA |
|---|---|---|
| **Pharma R&D hiring managers** | Download CV | View publications |
| **VCs/investors** | View platform (AtomBios) | Contact for pitch |
| **Academic collaborators** | View structural work | Email for projects |
| **Media/speaking** | View writing (Instagram) | Contact for interviews |

**The mistake:** Equal weight on everything = no clear action.

### 9. **What Claude Does Wrong (Anti-AI-Slop)**

From Anthropic's official frontend aesthetics guide:

**Claude's default convergent choices:**
- Inter, Roboto, Arial, system fonts
- Purple gradients on white
- Cookie-cutter layouts
- Evenly-distributed color palettes
- Solid backgrounds

**How to fight it:**
- **Typography:** High-contrast pairings (display + mono, serif + geometric sans). Extreme weights (200 vs 800, not 400 vs 600). Size jumps 3x+, not 1.5x.
- **Color:** Dominant color + sharp accent. Not timid pastels.
- **Backgrounds:** Atmospheric depth (layered gradients, patterns). Not solid white/gray.
- **Motion:** Orchestrated page load (staggered reveals). Not scattered micro-interactions.

**The test:** Would this design surprise and delight, or look like every other AI-generated site?

---

### 10. **Mobile-First Is Non-Negotiable**

**Reality check:** 60%+ of traffic is mobile. Pharma executives check sites on phones between meetings.

**What breaks on mobile:**
- Tiny click targets (<44px touch target)
- Horizontal scrolling
- Menus that require hover
- Text <16px (causes zoom)
- Images >1MB (slow load on LTE)

**What works:**
- Thumb-friendly navigation (bottom 1/3 of screen)
- Readable font sizes (18px+ for body text)
- Compressed images (WebP <200KB)
- Vertical scroll only
- One-column layouts

## Three Strategic Directions

Based on research, here are three distinct approaches:

### **Option A: The Scientist (Content-First Minimalism)**

**Inspiration:** Ben Kuhn, Patrick Collison, Seth Godin, Andrew Heiss

**Core principle:** Work speaks for itself. Design gets out of the way.

**Key elements:**
- Clean typography (serif for reading, mono for data/code)
- White/cream background, black text, one accent color
- Generous whitespace
- **Featured publications** with process notes (not just citations)
- **"Now" page:** What I'm working on this quarter
- **Recent updates** section (dated)
- Prominent CV download
- Direct email CTA

**Visual identity:**
- Refined but understated
- Think: academic journal meets personal notebook
- Typography = hierarchy (size, not color/animation)

**Best for:** Pharma R&D roles, academic positions, serious collaborators

**Risk:** Can feel boring if execution isn't tight

---

### **Option B: The Builder (Platform-First Narrative)**

**Inspiration:** Startup founders, Y Combinator alumni, product-led portfolios

**Core principle:** The platform (AtomBios) is the proof. Everything supports that story.

**Key elements:**
- **Hero:** AtomBios mission statement + visual (nanoparticle, structure, or data)
- **Platform section:** 20nm delivery explained with visuals
- **Proof points:** Funding, partnerships, data (BBB metrics, characterization)
- **Technical depth:** Cryo-EM structures, PDB deposits, methods
- **Team/Recognition:** Elite network, Lindau, Shaw Prize
- **Press/Media:** RTHK, Beijing TV, writing
- **Contact:** Investor pitch, collaboration inquiries

**Visual identity:**
- Modern, confident, data-driven
- Dark hero section with light content
- Data visualization aesthetic (charts, structure viewers)
- Professional but not corporate

**Best for:** VC meetings, industry partnerships, C-suite roles

**Risk:** Can overshadow personal credentials if not balanced

---

### **Option C: The Invisible Architect (Aesthetic Novelty)**

**Inspiration:** Your Instagram brand, award-winning portfolios, design-forward scientists

**Core principle:** The design reflects the beauty you see in molecular structures. Show, don't tell.

**Key elements:**
- **Visual-first:** Molecular structures, cryo-EM renders, PDB beauty shots
- **Story-driven sections:** Each area tells a narrative (SCALE, BUILD, GAP series)
- **Distinctive typography:** Not Inter/Roboto. High-contrast pairing.
- **Atmospheric backgrounds:** Gradients, depth, molecular patterns
- **Micro-interactions:** Hover states, staggered reveals, smooth transitions
- **Writing integrated:** Instagram posts embedded, long-form essays
- **Personal touch:** Photos from Lindau, Shaw Prize, lab work

**Visual identity:**
- Bold, memorable, surprising
- Think: Stripe meets Nature journal meets personal memoir
- Motion and atmosphere without gimmicks

**Best for:** Building personal brand, media/speaking, standing out in competitive fields

**Risk:** Can feel "designed" instead of serious if overdone

## Recommended Approach: **Hybrid — The Technical Storyteller**

**Why:** You need credibility (scientist) + differentiation (builder) + memorability (aesthetic).

**Strategy:**
1. **Structure:** Content-first (Option A foundation)
2. **Story:** Platform narrative (Option B framing)
3. **Aesthetic:** Distinctive but refined (Option C polish, not excess)

**Execution:**

### **Homepage (Above Fold)**
```
[Molecular structure background — subtle, dark]

Jackie Ng
Structural Biologist × Drug Delivery Engineer

I design nanoparticles that cross the blood-brain barrier.
Currently building a 20nm delivery platform at AtomBios.

[View Research] [Platform Overview] [Contact]
```

### **Content Sections**
1. **Now** — Current work (last updated: date)
2. **Platform** — AtomBios + proof (funding, data, partnerships)
3. **Publications** — Featured papers with artifacts (DOI, code, structures)
4. **Structural Work** — Cryo-EM gallery with PDB links
5. **Recognition** — Timeline with photos/context
6. **Writing** — Instagram embed or selected posts
7. **Talks** — Video/slides where available
8. **Contact** — Clear CTAs per audience type

### **Design Principles**
- **Typography:** Crimson Pro (serif, elegant) + Space Mono (technical data)
- **Color:** Dark navy/black + red accent (molecular = precision)
- **Backgrounds:** Atmospheric gradients + molecular texture overlays
- **Motion:** Staggered fade-ins on scroll, smooth hover states
- **Structure:** Single-page scroll OR multi-page with fixed nav

### **Proof of Life**
- "Last updated: [date]" in footer
- News/updates section (last 6 months)
- "Now" page (current quarter)

### **Mobile**
- Thumb-friendly nav (sticky bottom bar)
- Compressed images (<200KB)
- 18px+ body text
- Touch targets >44px

## Next Steps

### **Phase 1: Content Strategy (Do First)**
1. **Write "Now" section** — What are you working on right now?
2. **Select featured publications** — Which 3-5 papers best represent your work?
3. **Gather artifacts** — PDB codes, GitHub repos, datasets, project pages
4. **Draft platform story** — AtomBios in 3 paragraphs (problem → solution → proof)
5. **Collect visuals** — Molecular structures, lab photos, event photos (Lindau, Shaw Prize)
6. **Define CTAs** — What action for each audience type?

### **Phase 2: Design Execution**
1. **Choose typography** — High-contrast pairing (serif + mono or display + geometric sans)
2. **Define color system** — Primary + accent (avoid purple/generic blue)
3. **Create background treatment** — Atmospheric depth (gradients/patterns/molecular textures)
4. **Design component library** — Section headers, cards, buttons, links
5. **Build responsive grid** — Mobile-first, works 320px → 2560px

### **Phase 3: Build & Test**
1. **Code static HTML/CSS** (or use builder if speed matters)
2. **Optimize images** — WebP, <200KB per image
3. **Test mobile** — Real devices (iPhone, Android)
4. **Lighthouse audit** — Target >90 performance, accessibility, SEO
5. **Deploy** — GitHub Pages or custom domain

### **Phase 4: Maintain**
1. **Set reminder** — Update "Now" section monthly
2. **Add news items** — Publications, talks, awards as they happen
3. **Refresh annually** — New projects, updated CV, design tweaks
